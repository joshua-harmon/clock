

How the meeting point moves from one conjunction to the next. Each conjunction falls about 242.7° further along the zodiac, which is the same as stepping back 117.3°. So successive conjunctions trace a triangle (the trigon), and every third one comes back about 8.1° further ahead. The real conjunctions vary around that mean: from 1100 BC to AD 2050, single steps range from 219° to 263°, because the two orbits are oval.

How close they come. They’re never exactly on top of each other, because their orbits are tilted slightly against each other. At conjunction the gap varies:

Seen from the Sun: 0.02° to 1.35° (mean 0.81°).
Seen from Earth, at closest approach: 0.03° to 1.34°.

The recent ones swing in a three-step pattern, following the triangle: 2000 was 1.2°, 2020 was 0.11°, and 2040 will be 1.15°. One corner of the triangle sits near where the two orbits cross, so the conjunctions there are close (0.5° in 1901, 0.3° in 1961, 0.1° in 2020); the other two corners stay near 1°.

That’s why the separations at the 676 × 365 steps sit near a minimum and change only gradually: the step is almost a whole number of conjunctions, so the configuration nearly repeats.
